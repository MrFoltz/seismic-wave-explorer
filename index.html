How does the model work?

Each path follows Snell’s law in a round Earth. Rock gets stiffer with depth, so waves speed up as they go down, and that bends each path back toward the surface. At the core–mantle boundary (2,891 km deep) the rock becomes liquid: S waves stop, and P waves slow down and bend sharply toward the center.

This is a simplified Earth model tuned to match the classroom values (S waves stop at 103°). Real travel times vary a little from place to place, and the animation speed is faster than real time. The two waves keep their correct speeds relative to each other.
